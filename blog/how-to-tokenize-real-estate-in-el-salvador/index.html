How to tokenize real estate in El Salvador

El Salvador did something no other country in the Americas had done: it built a dedicated legal home for digital assets. The Digital Assets Issuance Law created a defined path for tokenizing real things — including real estate — and a regulator to walk it with, the National Commission of Digital Assets, known by its Spanish initials as the CNAD. For a property owner, that changes everything. Instead of asking whether tokenization is permitted in a grey area, you follow a written process, with a commission that reviews issuances and licenses the service providers who conduct them.

This guide is that process, step by step, for real estate in El Salvador — how Embodier runs it, with Salvadoran counsel, from the first conversation to the register. It is general information about the path, written for owners of Salvadoran hotels, resorts, buildings, and land. It is never legal advice: the lawyers of record are licensed in El Salvador, and their word governs any specific case.

Why El Salvador

Most countries still answer tokenization questions with rules written for something else. El Salvador wrote a law for the thing itself. The Digital Assets Issuance Law established the CNAD as the competent authority, set the framework under which digital asset service providers are licensed, and defined how issuances are prepared and reviewed. A property owner who tokenizes in El Salvador is not experimenting at the edge of the law — they are using a framework the country built on purpose, in the open, and maintains today.

The country's wider posture matters too. El Salvador speaks about digital assets at the level of national policy, its institutions answer questions about the framework directly, and its legal community has real, growing practice in it. When Embodier arranges a tokenization in El Salvador, the property walks a path that Salvadoran counsel knows step by step — because they walk it as their ordinary work.
