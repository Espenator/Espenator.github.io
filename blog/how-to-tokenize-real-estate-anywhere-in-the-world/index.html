How to tokenize real estate anywhere in the world

Tokenizing a property means taking a real asset — a hotel, a resort, a commercial building, a portfolio of homes — and recording ownership of it in digital form, on a register that approved participants can join. The building does not change. What changes is how ownership is held, recorded, and shared: instead of one name on one deed inside one filing cabinet, the ownership sits inside a legal structure built for the purpose, and the register records each participant's place in it.

This guide is the process, step by step, the way Embodier runs it for owners anywhere in the world. It is written for the person who owns the asset: the hotelier with a property that deserves a wider circle of owners, the family office holding buildings across borders, the developer who wants the register to open before the next project begins. Wherever your property stands, the path has the same shape — and it begins with a conversation, not a form.

What tokenization is, and what it is not

A token is a digital record of a right defined by legal documents. The documents come first: a holding structure, formed or adapted with licensed counsel where the property stands, holds the asset, and the token represents a place inside that structure. The register is the list of the people approved to hold those places, and technology keeps the register exact.

Tokenization is not a shortcut around the law of the place where the property stands. It is a way of organizing ownership that respects that law, works inside it, and uses it. Embodier operates as a marketplace with registrations in preparation in the jurisdictions where it works, and every property's path is documented before any register opens. Nothing here is a promise about any property's future. It is a process — careful, documented, and run in the open with the owner at every step.

Step three: property and title review

We review the property itself: its title, its ownership structure, its permits and standing, and anything that would make ownership hard to organize. Clean title is the foundation — a register can only record what the law already recognizes. Where something needs repair, the structuring plan names it, and local counsel advises on the remedy. Owners are sometimes surprised by what a careful review finds; better in week two than in month four.

Step four: valuation on real numbers

A tokenized property still stands or falls on its numbers, so the numbers get the bank treatment. The property is valued on its reconciled operating statements — two full years of them, reviewed to a bank standard, with the records matched against each other until they agree. This is the discipline Embodier applies to every asset it touches, including the ones it buys for its own collection. If the statements are not there, there is no number yet, and the plan says what to build so there can be one. A valuation built on real numbers is what lets serious participants take the register seriously.

Step five: legal structuring with local counsel

Licensed counsel in the property's jurisdiction forms or adapts the holding structure and prepares the legal path: the documents that define what the token represents, who may hold a place on the register, and how ownership moves when the venue for it opens. Embodier coordinates the path and the plan; the lawyers of record are licensed where the property stands, and their work is the work that makes the register real. This guide is general information about the process — it is never legal advice, and your counsel's word governs your case.

Step six: the regulatory path

Each jurisdiction has its own authority and its own sequence — a licensing framework for digital asset service providers in one country, a securities pathway in another, a commission that reviews issuances in a third. Whatever the shape, the sequence is respected: registrations are prepared with the competent authority, and the offering is documented before any register opens. Embodier's marketplace register is consistent everywhere: registrations in preparation until the authority of the place says otherwise, in writing.

Step seven: token design and setup

With the structure and the path in place, the token is designed around them: what it represents, the register sized to what the property can support, the records each participant will see in their own panel, and the rules that keep the register exact. The technology is the quiet part — permissioned, documented, and built so that a participant always knows what they hold and where it stands.

Step eight: the register and the launch

Approved participants join the register, and the property's story is told to the members who asked to hear it — the film, the place, the people who run it. A launch is a beginning, not an ending: the register is kept, the property is run beautifully, and the relationship with its owners continues on the same thread where it started.
