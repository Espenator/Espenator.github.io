The $100 Refundable Deposit: How Your Reservation Works

People ask about the reservation more than any other part of the program, so here it is, stated plainly.

Your reservation is a fully refundable $100 deposit, paid on Stripe. It is not a token purchase. It is not a commitment to invest, and it is not a fee. It is a good-faith marker that holds your place when we open bidding on a resort project. If you move forward with an offering, the deposit is credited toward your token purchase. At any time, for any reason, you may have it returned to you in full.

No fine print hides behind that paragraph. But the detail is worth walking through, because the reservation is the instrument that separates the curious from the serious — quietly, before any project is ever on the table.

A reservation is not a purchase

Most reservation systems in real estate are built to create pressure: a deposit that is difficult to recover, a clock that starts ticking, a sales office that calls twice a day. Ours is built to do the opposite.

The Embodier reservation exists for one reason. It lets you raise your hand for a specific project without risk, and it lets us see, before an offering opens, who is genuinely paying attention. Nothing more.

That distinction matters because the program is not selling urgency. We bring two or three resorts a year into the collection — a pace that makes patience part of the design. A collection is defined by what it keeps, and we keep the process as calm as the properties themselves.

The waitlist comes first

Before any reservation exists, there is the waitlist. Joining it costs nothing and obligates you to nothing. It is simply how you tell us you want to hear about upcoming projects — and how we keep the circle small enough to be real.

Waitlist members receive education about the program first: how tokenized ownership of a boutique resort works, what an offering looks like, what the timeline of a project is. No one is asked for money while they are learning. The waitlist is also the whitelist — when a project opens, members hear about it before anyone else, by text message.

This order is deliberate. Desire first, paperwork later. You decide a project is worth your attention; only then does the reservation enter the picture.

When the deposit comes into play

A reservation opens only when we bring a specific resort into the program and present an offering to the circle. At that point, waitlist members are invited to place a reservation: a fully refundable $100 deposit, paid on Stripe.

The deposit does two quiet jobs. First, it holds your place among the people who have raised their hands for that project. Second, it signals that your interest is real enough to mark — not a commitment to fund, simply a sign that you are paying attention.

Nothing is collected at signup, and nothing is collected while you are simply on the waitlist. The deposit appears at exactly one moment: when there is a real project, with real documents, and you want us to know you are serious about it.

What your reservation does while you wait

Once placed, your reservation sits as a credit in your name, attached to the project you marked. It does not expire while the offering is being prepared. It does not convert into anything you did not ask for. It simply holds your place.

If the project moves forward and you decide to participate, the deposit is credited toward your token purchase at the offering — it becomes part of your allocation, not an added cost. If you decide the project is not for you, the deposit is returned to you in full. If the project itself does not move forward, the same applies: it comes back.

In every scenario except one, the deposit never becomes a sunk cost. It is a marker, not a payment.

Fully refundable means fully refundable

Refundable, in this program, is not a marketing word. It is the operating rule.

You may request your deposit back at any time, for any reason, and it is returned in full. There are no forms to argue over, no retention windows, no partial credit, no questions about your reasons. A reservation designed to make raising your hand easy cannot have a difficult exit — that would defeat its own purpose.

This is also why the deposit is small. It is large enough to mean something, and small enough that no one needs to think twice about it. The seriousness it measures is attention, not wealth.

The one exception

There is exactly one circumstance in which the deposit is not returned, and it is worth stating clearly.

Placing a reservation is not a commitment to fund. But later in the process, when offering documents are final and allocations are being confirmed, you may be asked to make a formal commitment to fund a specific allocation. That commitment is a separate instrument, entered deliberately, with its own paperwork.

If you make that formal commitment and then do not follow through, the deposit is kept as liquidated damages — the entire deposit, and nothing beyond it. That is the full extent of the commitment's teeth: exactly the deposit, never more. Everything before that point — the waitlist, the learning, the reservation itself — carries no such consequence.

What exists today

Honesty about timing is part of the program. Today, four things exist: the waitlist, education about how the program works, soft commitments from people who want to be notified, and the reservation program for upcoming projects.

No token sale is open today, and no trading is taking place. When a secondary market for issued tokens arrives, it will arrive under license. Unlocks with our pending El Salvador license approval.

Until then, the reservation is the instrument that lets serious participants mark their place. The offering documents, the identity verification, and the subscription process arrive with each project, in their own time — and only then does anything beyond the deposit come into play.

Who the reservation is for

The reservation is for people who collect. People who have stayed somewhere extraordinary and thought: the resort's mine — want a piece? People who understand that two or three exceptional resorts a year is a pace worth waiting for, because scarcity is what makes a collection a collection.

It is not for speculators. Not a coin. Not ayahuasca theater. The program is built for long-hold ownership of real, operating boutique resorts — places with staff who know your name and rooms you return to. The reservation simply lets you raise your hand early, quietly, and without risk.

If that describes you, the path is simple. Join the waitlist, learn the program at your own pace, and place a reservation when a project you love comes into view. No one will chase you. That is not how we operate.
